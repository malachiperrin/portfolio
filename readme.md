## Featured Work

### 01 · Satisfaction Survey Email Flow Redesign

**Self-directed proof of concept · Retail operations · 2026**

A national print-and-shipping retailer sent one "How did we do?" survey email after every transaction. Customers picking up held packages had no way to indicate which party they were rating, so retail locations absorbed blame for delivery delays caused upstream. The delivery network never learned about its own failures.

I mapped the existing flow, identified where attribution breaks down, and designed a replacement sequence: a context-setting initial email, a reminder cadence with suppression logic, distinct paths for positive and negative feedback, complaint routing by category, status updates, and a resolution flow with a reopen window.

The issue was raised internally, acknowledged, and never acted on. I designed the fix on my own.

**Skills:** Journey mapping · Email flow design · Systems thinking · Consent and privacy handling
**Tools:** Figma, Canva
**Status:** Design concept. Not implemented.

### 02 · Pizzeria Brand and Web Package

**Client project · Branding, print, and web · Budget: $800**

A local pizzeria needed printed materials and an online presence with a hard ceiling of $800. A custom site with integrated checkout was not possible at that number, so the entire project was scoped around that constraint.

Deliverables included business cards and an 18x24 inch promotional poster designed in Figma and Canva, plus a 4-to-5 page mobile-first website covering Home, Menu, About, Locations, and Contact. Online ordering routes through third-party platforms (UberEats, GrubHub, DoorDash) rather than a custom checkout. Email marketing was recommended on Klaviyo or Mailchimp instead of a proprietary system.

Client name and logo were replaced with AI-generated alternatives for confidentiality.

**Skills:** Print design · Web design · Scoping under budget constraints · Platform recommendations
**Tools:** Figma, Canva
**Status:** Delivered.

## How I Work

**Start with the symptom, trace to the cause.** A bad review, a low conversion rate, a confusing form. The visible problem is rarely the actual problem.

**Respect the budget.** Constraints are design inputs, not obstacles. A solution the client cannot maintain is not a solution.

**Close the loop.** Every flow, form, and interaction should tell the user what happens next.
